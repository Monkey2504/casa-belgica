Leave the Squat, a pilot project run by Ballal ASBL (company no. 1016.925.333), in Molenbeek: one building, Casa Belgica, 50 rooms at €300 a month.

Nobody ever buys their home in cash.

In Brussels, someone leaving an occupied building goes from a rent of €0 to a rent of €600 to €720 for a room; a studio costs €885. On a small wage, or on CPAS support for those entitled to it (€1,367 a month), nothing is left to put aside. And landlords ask for an income of three times the rent and for payslips: the person does not even get the lease. That is the problem.

Our solution: obtain an empty building on a very long lease (an emphyteutic lease, thirty years at least), renovate it, and rent out rooms at €300 a month, with a real lease. That is the solution.

Like everyone else, we will take out a loan. The pilot costs €1.72 million. A €0.72 million loan is repaid by the rents. One million remains to be found: €500,000 in small loans from citizens and €500,000 in donations. Your €1,000 loan is pledged today, paid only once the building is found and the financing granted. The problem

Between €0 and €720, a player is missing.

In Brussels, someone leaving an occupied building goes from a rent of €0 to a rent of €600 to €720 for a room. A studio costs €885. In between there is almost nothing: a few dozen rooms in non-profit projects.

The project targets a specific group: those who can pay a rent, but to whom nobody will rent.

Landlords ask for an income equal to three times the rent; the Council of State upheld this practice on 30 March 2026. For a €720 room you need €2,160 in income, and payslips you do not yet have when you have just been regularised.

A squat makes it possible to live cheaply. What it does not give: a secure right to occupy the premises, hence a stable address, hence access to rights, and the certainty of not being evicted before you have finished saving.

We live at 645 chaussée de Gand, in Molenbeek, a former care home occupied since November 2023, where about 130 people live. Ballal ASBL was born there and holds its drop-in there; it supports more than 150 people. The project was born there, out of a discussion between Telly and François.

62,234 households were waiting for social housing on 31 December 2025, with a wait of 9 to 13 years depending on the type of home.

What we are building

50 rooms, one lease per person, €300 a month.

Rooms of 18 m², each with shower and toilet, at €300 a month all included: heating, water, electricity, internet. A building of about 1,350 m², lent by a public or institutional owner on a very long lease (an emphyteutic lease) of at least thirty years. We have neither a building nor a quote yet.

The building is divided into small groups of 6 to 10 rooms, by floor or by wing. Each group shares a kitchen and a living room, and nothing else: no shared budget, shopping or meals. One main-residence lease per room, a letterbox, a doorbell, a registered address.

Who for. People who have just been regularised, who are leaving an occupied building and who have an income, first of all from work. What counts to get in: being able to pay €300 a month, whether the income comes from a wage or from support.

Four legal conditions

  1. Single-person status, for residents on CPAS supportIndividual leases, no shared kitty or meals, and a written opinion from the CPAS before opening. Residents who work are not affected.
  2. Collective housing, neither a centre nor a shelterTo be recognised as solidarity housing (habitat solidaire) under the Brussels Housing Code.
  3. The planning permitA change of use requires a permit: 12 to 24 months before the works.
  4. Fire safetyMandatory opinion from the fire service (SIAMU). We estimate this item at €110,000 to €430,000 for 50 rooms.

What the rents repay, and when

Out of €14,250 in rents a month, €8,120 keeps the building running and €4,176 repays the bank. That leaves €1,204 to €1,954 a month for citizen lenders. Each year, this money repays in full those who ask, in the order of the requests. If no new lender ever came, the rents alone would repay everyone between year 21 and year 26, before the end of a thirty-year lease. That is the worst case, not the promise.

Our limit is one million to find. A building to be fully converted would need 2 to 2.4 million, a building bought 3.5 to 3.9: we will not launch the pilot with those.

The down payment

Five sources, none is enough on its own.

  1. A building lent for a long timeAn emphyteutic lease: the association renovates at its own expense, uses the building, then hands it back at the end. The fee can be zero. This is the biggest contribution: there is no building to buy.
  2. Long, low-interest loansLenders whose mission is to finance social projects, more cheaply and over longer periods than a conventional bank.
  3. Citizen loans500 loans of €1,000 on average, from €500, at 0 to 1.5% interest as the lender chooses, with six months' notice to take the money back. Belgian law allows this without a heavy procedure: less than €500,000 a year, and no more than €5,000 per lender. Our €500,000 will therefore be raised in two rounds.
  4. Events and donationsConcerts, parties, sponsored runs. Our estimate: around €200,000 a year. This pays for equipment and start-up, not for the building.
  5. Three public decisionsLower taxes on the building, a regional allowance paid to the resident, and outcome-based payment for social support.

Your €1,000 is a pledge, not a transfer. Not a euro is asked for before the building is found and the financing granted. After that, it is a loan: you take it back with six months' notice, and you are repaid by the rents or by a new lender who takes your place.

The wall on this page has 120 bricks, i.e. €120,000: the first of the €500,000 in citizen loans.

What the rent does not pay for either: social support. The budget pays for a half-time manager, not social workers. We are looking for partners whose job this already is.

Objections

The questions we will be asked.

Who decides?

Ballal ASBL holds the emphyteutic lease, the loan and the citizen loans. No profit is distributed and the building cannot be resold. The residents decide: a majority of votes at the general assembly, half the seats on the board, one person, one vote.

When is my loan repaid?

You can take it back with six months' notice. Each year, the rents repay some lenders in full, in the order of the requests; the others are replaced by new lenders. If too many requests come at once, everyone waits their turn. In the worst case, with no new lender at all, the rents repay everyone between year 21 and year 26. The building only borrows what its own rents can repay before the end of the lease. If the project goes bankrupt, you come after the bank and you can lose your money.

Why one more player?

Because between €0 and €720 there is almost nobody. Social housing has 62,234 households waiting, for 9 to 13 years. Social rental agencies have few rooms for single people. We do not want to replace anyone: we want to fill the gap, and build it with those who already do the rest.

And people who do not have papers yet?

They are not Casa Belgica's public: the project relies on a rent paid from a regular income, and without a residence permit you cannot work legally. They are Ballal's public, and Ballal keeps supporting them where they live, in the occupied buildings.

Where do residents go after three years?

To a flat in their own name. With a full-time job and a €300 rent, a person can save €10,000 in under two years; part-time, it takes the three years. We will help them look. Nobody is put out.

What are the risks?

Works costing more than planned, the VAT rate on the works (6, 12 or 21% depending on the building), the permit (12 to 24 months), no public owner agreeing. Our answer: four steps, each able to stop the project, and €30,000 committed at most before the final decision.
