The problem

Leaving the squat — and then what?

Someone who has just obtained their papers receives an integration income (RIS) at the single rate: €1,340/month. A studio in Brussels costs €725 in rent, plus the rental deposit. That leaves €615 for everything else. No savings, never a deposit, and the first setback leads straight back to the street.

Between the squat and that studio, there is nothing.

A squat already makes it possible to live cheaply. What it does not give: a secure right of occupancy — hence an address, hence a domiciliation, hence access to rights — and the certainty of not being evicted before having finished saving.

Ballal ASBL has supported more than 150 undocumented or precarious people in Molenbeek since 2015. Mothers, children, whole families, moved from one squat to another over the years: they are the ones who asked for something other than one more shelter, a stable building, their own. Sortir du squat was born from that request, carried by Ballal since 2023.

At the end of 2025, 62,234 households are waiting for social housing in Brussels — around 10% of Brussels households — with a 9 to 13 year wait depending on the type of housing. Between 2020 and 2024, the Region invested close to 2 billion euros, built more than 2,000 homes and renovated more than 7,500 — and demand rose by 25% over the same period.

What we are building

50 rooms, €300 a month, all included.

50 individual rooms of at least 18 m² net, each with its own shower and toilet. An old Brussels building of around 1,200 m², under an emphyteutic lease from a public or institutional owner — target: 30 years maximum, needed to pay off the million euros invested in the works. Nothing is signed yet; the law allows up to 99 years, but it is this 30-year duration that the case's financial projections are based on.

No purchase: at €300/month, the model cannot carry any acquisition. Since the 2021 reform of the Civil Code, a free non-profit emphyteutic lease in favour of an ASBL is explicitly permitted. The ground rent can therefore be nil.

The individual shower and toilet are not a comfort. They secure single-person status with the CPAS — €447/month of extra income per resident — and therefore the ability to pay €300. We never economise on this item.

Repayment

Why this is not a donation.

Legal form: subordinated loan at 0%, with no repayment during the first five years, repaid in one instalment between year 6 and year 10. Subordinated means: in a crisis, the bank comes before the citizens. That is exactly what allows the bank to treat the €120,000 as quasi-equity and to keep the leverage at €900,000.

You are funding a model, not a single building. Sortir du squat is a programme; Casa Belgica is its first building. Once repaid, the €120,000 goes into the second one, with the same set-up: articles of association, emphyteutic lease, bank file, all of it serves the next.

If the bank refuses the subordination, the €120,000 becomes cooperative shares — equity with no exit. The leverage and the budget stay the same, but the money does not come back.

Objections

What we hear most often.

Who decides — the lenders?

The residents decide. The lenders bring the capital; the residents hold the majority of votes at the general meeting and half the seats on the board. One person, one vote.

Why not an AIS, social housing, or a CLTB project?

Social housing has 62,234 applicants at the end of 2025, with a 9 to 13 year wait. The CLTB does acquisitions for households, with more than 1,300 people on the waiting list. Other players create group housing with building ownership — Fair Ground Brussels, L'Îlot, Renovassistance.

What nobody offers today: a three-to-four-year stepping stone, at €300 all included, in an individual room with single-person status, for people leaving squats. It is that combination that is missing — not social housing.

50 places is not many, and the first key comes in three years.

50 places with stays of three to four years is 250 to 350 people over twenty years — from a single building, and the citizen capital goes on to a second one. Nothing is on hold in the meantime: the occupations continue, the support continues.

And if it fails?

Entry costs are capped at €30,000, largely eligible for subsidy, and the project stops cleanly at four defined points, before a single citizen euro moves.
